How school term dates work in the UK

01

Set locally, not nationally

Each local education authority publishes a calendar for the community and voluntary-controlled schools in its area, shaped around 190 teaching days, bank holidays and local consultation — which is why the school year starts on a Tuesday in one county and a Thursday in the next. Academies and free schools technically set their own dates, though most stay within a day or two of their council's pattern.

02

Published a year ahead

Councils usually publish a year or more ahead: most confirm the following September's dates by the previous autumn, and Welsh authorities coordinate more closely with each other than English ones do. Every page in this directory is rebuilt from the council's own published calendar and re-checked monthly, with a link to the original source at the bottom.

03

INSET days are the catch

The dates families actually get caught out by are usually not in the council calendar at all: INSET days are picked school by school, and the odd school shifts a term boundary. That is why the calendar on the school newsletter — and the one on your fridge — matters as much as the official list.

Common questions

Your school's own published dates are always the final word.

Are these school term dates official?

Yes - every page is built from the relevant council's own published term-date calendar, with a link to the source at the bottom. We re-check each council monthly. Your school's own published dates are always the final word, since individual schools can add INSET days or vary the odd date.

Why do neighbouring councils have different term dates?

Each local authority consults on and sets its own calendar, so two councils a few miles apart can place half term or the Easter break a week apart. It usually comes down to how they fit 190 teaching days around bank holidays and local traditions - and it is why families near a council boundary should always check the right council, not the nearest one.

Do academies, free schools and private schools follow these dates?

Not automatically. Council term dates formally apply to community and voluntary-controlled schools. Academies, free schools and foundation schools set their own calendars - though most stay close to the council's to keep life simple for families - and independent schools run noticeably different, usually longer, holidays. Always confirm with the school itself.

What is an INSET day?

An in-service training day: the school is open for staff but closed to pupils. Every state school in England and Wales takes five a year, chosen by the school rather than the council - which is why they rarely appear in council calendars and catch so many families out. Schools confirm them in newsletters, usually a term at a time.

Which parts of the UK does this directory cover?

The 176 local education authorities across England and Wales - the bodies that publish term dates this way. Scotland and Northern Ireland set school holidays through separate systems, so their dates are not listed here yet.
