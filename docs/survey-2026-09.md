# User survey, September 2026

Status: DRAFT for the founder to build in Tally (free tier has branching,
URL prefill and no ads) and send via a personalised Postmark script, the
same mechanism as `web/scripts/send-winback.mjs`.

## Why this shape

Three rules kept it short and useful:

1. **Ask only what the database cannot tell us.** We already know who linked
   WhatsApp, who connected a calendar, who used the assistant. Only the
   person knows *why they didn't*, so every question targets a why.
2. **Every question maps to a decision.** Is the blocker the product or the
   price; is a feature undiscovered, unconvincing or broken; who should we
   interview.
3. **Branching, so nobody sees a question that doesn't apply.** Most people
   answer about ten questions in three to four minutes.

The single most important question is Q3, the "how would you feel if you
could no longer use it" measure. Forty percent or more answering "very
disappointed" is the conventional product-market-fit line. The most
diagnostic is Q4, the feature grid, because its four columns separate
discoverability problems from value problems from product problems.

## Incentive

- **30 days of Premium for every completion.** Costs nothing: it is one
  update to `households.complimentary_until`. Fourteen days is too little
  to move anyone; thirty is the floor.
- **One £100 voucher, drawn from all completions.** This is the part that
  reaches lapsed users. Premium days are worth nothing to someone who has
  stopped using the app, and those are exactly the people we most need to
  hear from.
- **"I read every answer myself."** At 155 households the personal ask
  from the founder is the strongest lever there is. Say it and mean it.

## Build notes

- Tally link carries `?email=<email>&seg=<active|lapsed>` so responses join
  back to accounts and the interview question can be prefilled. Tally maps
  URL params to hidden fields.
- Two email variants (below), chosen by `seg`. One reminder to

- Reward fulfilment: after close, set `complimentary_until = now() + 30
  days` for each responding household. One script; write it when the

## The 15-minute interview (Q14 takers)

The survey says what happened; the call is for why, and the story around
it. Ask about their life and what they have actually done, never their
opinion of the product or what they would want in future. Five questions
with follow-ups fits in fifteen minutes.

**Before the call.** Open the household in admin: signup date, WhatsApp
linked, calendar connected, last active, what they created. Ask about
specifics ("you added term dates but never a meal plan, what happened?")
rather than generalities.

1. **Their setup (2 min).** Who is in the family, ages, who organises.
   Where does family information live today: WhatsApp group, paper
   calendar, shared Google calendar, their head. This is the real
   competition.
2. **The moment they signed up (3 min).** What was going on that day?
   Where did they hear about it? What did they hope it would do? The
   trigger is the marketing message.
3. **The first two days (3 min).** What did they do first, where did they
   get stuck. Then the key question: what did your partner say when you
   told them? Whether the second adult joined predicts retention better
   than anything else.
4. **Now (3 min).** Last time they opened it, what for? What do they still
   do outside Housemait that they expected to do in it? Lapsed: what was
   the last thing they did before stopping, and was there a moment they
   gave up?
5. **Money (2 min).** Have they ever paid for a family or organising app,
   and what made them? Past behaviour only. Never "would you pay £5.99";
   instead "what would have to be true for it to be worth that?"
6. **Close (1 min).** Who else do you know with this problem? Can I email
   you if I build something from this?

**Running it.** Ask to record, then listen; talk about a fifth of the
time. Follow-ups are "tell me more", "what happened then", "when was the
last time that happened". Never defend or explain a feature; a
misunderstanding is a finding. Write down exact phrases (they become
copy). Do not ask what features they would like. Do five to eight calls
before drawing conclusions.
